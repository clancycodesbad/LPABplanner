# Requirements

> Generated by [Quality Playbook](https://github.com/andrewstellman/quality-playbook) v1.5.6 — Andrew Stellman
> Date: 2026-09-26 · Project: LPABplanner

## Overview

LPABplanner is a free, community-built, single-page web tool that helps students enrolled in the NSW Legal Profession Admission Board's (LPAB) Diploma in Law map out which of the Diploma's 20 required subjects they will take in which semester, track their progress toward the 17-compulsory-plus-3-elective graduation requirement, and avoid enrolling in two subjects that share a lecture night or an exam time slot. It runs entirely in the browser with no server and no account system — a student's plan lives only in that browser's `localStorage`.

The actors are: **the student** (the only end user — drags subjects between a "pool" of available subjects and semester cards, marks subjects complete, exports their plan, and occasionally resets it), and **the volunteer maintainer** (updates `subjects.js`/`archive.js` twice a year when LPAB releases a new timetable, per the README's documented procedure — this person may not be a programmer, and the update procedure is designed to be followed by copy-pasting values from a published PDF). The Diploma in Law is a real, long-running distance-education pathway to legal practice in NSW administered by the University of Sydney's Law Extension Committee on behalf of LPAB; students using this tool are making genuine multi-year enrollment decisions, and a clash the tool fails to catch is a real scheduling conflict the student discovers too late to fix.

The highest-risk areas, grounded in Phase 1 exploration, are: (1) the two independently-implemented drag-and-drop code paths (mouse vs. touch) that can silently lose a subject from the plan on a failed move; (2) the exam-clash detector's scope gap, which only checks the single "current" term even though the tool's core purpose is multi-year forward planning; and (3) the hand-maintained, twice-yearly data files (`subjects.js`, `archive.js`, `data/stats/*.js`) that a non-programmer volunteer edits directly, with several code paths (date parsing, stats registry) that have no defensive validation against plausible transcription mistakes.

## Overview validation gate

- Names the project's ecosystem role and real-world significance: yes — a real, ongoing legal-education pathway (LPAB Diploma in Law) with genuine enrollment stakes.
- Identifies who depends on it and for what: yes — students (planning) and a volunteer maintainer (twice-yearly data updates).
- Would a daily user recognize this description: yes — matches the README's own framing exactly ("map out their 20-subject progression... tracks progress... detects... clashes").
- Reflects known adoption: this is a niche, single-community tool (not a widely-adopted OSS library), so there is no external adoption signal to cite beyond the README's own description of its userbase; the overview instead grounds itself in the real-world domain (LPAB/Diploma in Law) it serves. **PASS** for this project's scale.

## Use Cases

- **UC-01** — *New student sets up their plan via the suggested pathway.* Actor: student. A first-time visitor completes the onboarding wizard choosing "Starting fresh," reviews the LPAB-recommended 8-semester sequence, and applies it, ending with most core/compulsory subjects pre-placed and elective slots left for them to fill in later.
- **UC-02** — *Student rebalances their plan by dragging a subject to a different semester.* Actor: student. A returning student with an existing plan drags an already-placed subject from one semester card to another (e.g., because a semester became too full or their availability changed), and the subject ends up in the new semester without being lost.
- **UC-03** — *Student tracks graduation progress.* Actor: student. As subjects are added and marked complete, the compulsory/elective progress bars and the "ready to graduate" status update to reflect exactly the LPAB requirement (17 compulsory + 3 electives).
- **UC-04** — *Student resolves a lecture-night or exam-time clash.* Actor: student. Two subjects placed in the same semester that share a lecture night (or, for the current term, an exact exam time) are visibly flagged, and moving one elsewhere clears the warning.
- **UC-05** — *Student checks a subject's exam date before committing.* Actor: student. Before finalizing a semester's subjects, the student reads the exam date shown on each tile — either the live date (current term), the subject's own future-term date, or the most recent historical sitting.
- **UC-06** — *Student exports their plan for use outside the app.* Actor: student. The student copies a markdown-formatted rendition of their entire plan (completed + every planned semester) to the clipboard for pasting into a notes app.
- **UC-07** — *Student resets their plan without losing preferences.* Actor: student. Partway through planning, the student clears all placed/completed subjects via the two-step confirmation modal while keeping their hidden-subject preferences intact, or alternatively wipes everything and re-runs onboarding.
- **UC-08** — *Maintainer updates the app for a new LPAB semester.* Actor: volunteer maintainer. Following the README's documented procedure, the maintainer edits only `subjects.js` (lecture/exam/terms/currentTerm) and `archive.js` (archiving the outgoing semester) — no other file needs to change for the app to reflect the new timetable.

## Requirements by Functional Section

### Semester Validation and Enrollment Rules

Governs whether a subject may be placed into a given semester at all — term availability and the 4-subjects-per-semester cap.

**REQ-001** — Term availability enforcement
- Tier: 3 (source-of-truth code)
- Summary: A subject must not be addable to a semester whose season (winter/summer, derived from the semester ID) is not listed in the subject's `terms` array.
- User story: As a student picking a semester for an elective, I expect the planner to block the add and tell me why **so that** I don't plan a subject into a term it doesn't run in.
- Implementation: `engine.js:8-13` (`isAvailableInTerm`), consumed by `validateSemester` (`engine.js:15-28`).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) A Winter-only subject (e.g. id `16`) is rejected for any `summerYYYY` semester with an explanatory error naming the subject and the term. (b) A Summer-only subject (e.g. id `18`) is rejected for any `winterYYYY` semester. (c) A subject offered in both terms is accepted in either. (d) `termId === 'completed'` always passes this check regardless of the subject's `terms`.
- Alternative paths: Bypassed entirely for `semesterId === 'completed'` (`planner-state.js:77`, C-039) — a subject can be marked completed even if it was never offered in a term the student took it (reasonable, since "completed" isn't tied to a specific semester in this data model).
- Use cases: UC-01, UC-02, UC-08
- References: `engine.js:8-28`; README "Updating for a New Semester" step 3 (Tier 4).

**REQ-002** — Semester capacity limit
- Tier: 3
- Summary: A semester (any non-`completed` term key) must reject a new subject once it already holds 4 subjects.
- User story: As a student overloading a semester, I expect to be blocked at the 5th subject **so that** my plan reflects a realistic courseload.
- Implementation: `engine.js:23-25`.
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) Adding a 5th subject to a semester with 4 already placed returns an error and does not mutate the semester. (b) Adding a 4th subject to a semester with 3 succeeds. (c) The `completed` bucket has no such cap (verified: `addSubject` skips `validateSemester` entirely for `completed`, per REQ-001's alternative path).
- Use cases: UC-01, UC-02
- References: `engine.js:23-25`.

**REQ-003** — Duplicate-subject prevention
- Tier: 3
- Summary: A subject already present anywhere in the plan (any semester, or completed) must not be addable again.
- User story: As a student who already placed a subject, I expect a repeat drag onto any semester to be rejected with a clear "already in your plan" message **so that** I never end up with the same subject double-counted toward my progress.
- Implementation: `planner-state.js:70-73` (`addSubject`, checked against `getProgress().totalSubjectsList`).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) Dragging a subject already in `winter2026` onto `summer2026` is rejected. (b) Dragging a subject already marked `completed` onto any semester is rejected. (c) The rejection message names the subject.
- Use cases: UC-02
- References: `planner-state.js:69-92`.

### Core Subject Sequencing

Warns (non-blocking) when a core subject (IDs 01–11) is placed before an earlier core subject is anywhere in the plan.

**REQ-004** — Core-order advisory warning
- Tier: 3
- Summary: Placing a core subject while an earlier-sequence core subject is missing from the plan, or planned for a later semester, must produce a non-blocking warning naming those prerequisite(s), never an error that prevents the placement. A prerequisite that is completed or in the same or an earlier semester is in order. The warning stays on the subject's tile until it is resolved.
- User story: As a student planning core subjects out of the recommended order, I expect a warning explaining that LPAB approval is required **so that** I'm informed without being stopped outright (LPAB does sometimes grant such approval).
- Implementation: `Engine.coreOrderIssues` (engine.js) finds missing and later prerequisites; `Engine.checkCoreOrder` builds the message. Invoked from `planner-state.js` `addSubject` (post-add, for the returned `warnings` array), pre-emptively from `ui-board.js` `handleAddSubject` (feedback panel), and from `ui-board.js` `createSubjectSlot` on every render (the lasting tile warning).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) Adding core subject `05` (Real Property) when `01`–`04` are not yet anywhere in the plan produces a warning naming Foundations of Law, Criminal Law & Procedure, Torts, and Contracts. (b) Adding core subject `01` (first in sequence) never produces this warning. (c) A non-core subject (compulsory or elective) never produces this warning. (d) The warning never blocks the add — `addSubject` still returns `success: true`. (e) A prerequisite planned for a later semester is flagged; one in the same semester or completed is not. (f) The tile warning clears as soon as the prerequisites are in order.
- Alternative paths: The same check runs twice per add (once in `ui-board.js` for the pre-emptive banner, once inside `planner-state.js` for the returned result) — both must agree, since they call the identical `Engine.checkCoreOrder` function with the same arguments.
- Use cases: UC-01
- References: `engine.js:30-62`.

### Clash Detection

Detects lecture-night and exam-time conflicts between subjects placed in the same semester.

**REQ-005** — Lecture-night clash detection (all semesters)
- Tier: 3
- Summary: Any two subjects placed in the same semester that share an identical `lecture` value must both be flagged with a lecture-clash warning, for every semester (not scoped to the current term).
- User story: As a student who has placed two Tuesday-lecture subjects in the same semester, I expect both tiles to show a clash warning **so that** I know to move one before I'm stuck attending two lectures at once.
- Implementation: `engine.js:64-79` (`getClashingSubjects`, lecture branch — lines 72-79, unconditional on `semesterId`).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) Two subjects with `lecture: 'Tuesday'` in the same semester both receive a `"Lecture clash on Tuesday"` warning. (b) Subjects with different lecture days in the same semester receive no lecture warning. (c) This check runs identically whether the semester is the current term or any future term.
- Use cases: UC-04
- References: `engine.js:64-92`.

**REQ-006** — Exam-time clash detection (current term only) — flagged completeness gap
- Tier: 3
- Summary: Two subjects placed in the same semester whose exam date-times are exactly equal must be flagged with an exam-clash warning — **but this check only executes when the semester being evaluated equals `currentTerm`** (`engine.js:81`).
- User story: As a student planning subjects into the current term, I expect exam-time clashes to be flagged **so that** I don't accidentally sit two exams at once. *(As a student planning subjects into a future term, I currently get no such protection — see Conditions of satisfaction (d) below and CB-5/BUG tracker.)*
- Implementation: `engine.js:64-92`, specifically the `semesterId === currentTerm` guard at line 81.
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) Two subjects with identical `exam` strings placed in the semester equal to `currentTerm` both receive an exam-clash warning. (b) Two subjects with different exam times in the current term receive no exam warning. (c) A subject with a `null` exam never contributes to a clash (per `examTimesClash`'s null-guard, REQ-009). (d) **[Currently unmet by design/oversight]** Two subjects with identical exam times placed in a semester that is NOT `currentTerm` (e.g. a future term the student is planning ahead into) receive **no** exam-clash warning at all, even though the same two subjects would be flagged if placed in the current term instead. This condition is recorded as the documented *actual* behavior for traceability; whether it should instead behave like REQ-005 (unscoped) is Phase 3/4's determination — see BUGS.md.
- Alternative paths: The lecture-clash branch (REQ-005) and the exam-clash branch of the *same function* have different term-scoping — this asymmetry within one function is the basis of the completeness concern.
- Use cases: UC-04
- References: `engine.js:64-92`.

**REQ-007** — Exam date parsing strictness
- Tier: 4 (README explicitly documents the strict format) / Tier 3 (implementation)
- Summary: `parseExamDate` must accept exactly the format `'D Mon YYYY, H.MM am/pm'` (case-insensitive am/pm, 1–2 digit day/hour) and must return `null` (with a console warning) for anything else, including subtly malformed variants.
- User story: As a maintainer transcribing exam dates from a published PDF, I expect the app to warn (in the console) if I mistype the format **so that** data-entry mistakes are at least theoretically detectable — *though see Conditions of satisfaction (d) for the display-layer gap that undermines this in practice.*
- Implementation: `js/utils/datetime.js:23-53`.
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) `'8 Sep 2026, 9.00 am'` parses to the correct `Date`. (b) `'7 Sep 2026, 1.45 pm'` parses with correct 24-hour conversion (13:45). (c) A string with an unrecognized month (e.g. `'8 Sept 2026, 9.00 am'`) returns `null` and logs a warning. (d) **The display layer (`getExamText`, REQ-020) never calls this parser and will show a malformed string verbatim as if it were valid** — this is the CB-2 finding; recorded here as a specification note connecting the two requirements.
- Use cases: UC-05, UC-08
- References: `js/utils/datetime.js:1-53`; README "Exam date format is strict" (Tier 4).

**REQ-008** — Exam-time equality is exact
- Tier: 3
- Summary: `examTimesClash` returns `true` only when both exam date-times are identical to the minute; near-miss times (e.g., 15 minutes apart) are never flagged.
- User story: As a student with two exams 15 minutes apart, I understand the tool will not flag this as a clash **so that** I know I must judge timing overlaps myself for anything short of an exact match.
- Implementation: `js/utils/datetime.js:85-89`.
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) Two identical exam strings clash. (b) Two exam strings differing by any amount of time (even 1 minute) do not clash. (c) Either input being unparseable/null never produces a clash (safe default).
- Use cases: UC-04
- References: `js/utils/datetime.js:85-89`.

**REQ-009** — Null-safe clash inputs
- Tier: 3
- Summary: A subject with `exam: null` must never be reported as clashing with anything, and must never cause an exception in the clash-detection pipeline.
- User story: As a student with an elective that has no exam this term, I expect no false clash warnings involving that subject **so that** exam-free electives don't pollute my clash list.
- Implementation: `js/utils/datetime.js:85-89` combined with `engine.js:81`.
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) A subject with `exam: null` paired with any other subject (including another `exam: null` subject) in the current term never produces an exam-clash warning. (b) No exception is thrown.
- Use cases: UC-04, UC-05
- References: `js/utils/datetime.js:85-89`.

### Progress Tracking and Graduation Status

**REQ-010** — Compulsory/elective progress counts
- Tier: 3
- Summary: The progress tracker must count exactly the subjects whose `type` (case-insensitive) is `'compulsory'` or `'elective'`, across every semester including `completed`.
- User story: As a student, I expect my progress bars to reflect every subject I've placed anywhere in my plan, not just completed ones **so that** I can see my full trajectory, not just what's already finished.
- Implementation: `engine.js:94-108` (`calculateProgress`).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) A subject placed in a future semester (not yet completed) counts toward progress immediately upon being added. (b) A subject in `completed` also counts. (c) Compulsory and elective counts are tracked independently.
- Use cases: UC-03
- References: `engine.js:94-108`.

**REQ-011** — Graduation-ready determination
- Tier: 3
- Summary: `readyToGraduate` must be `true` if and only if exactly 17 compulsory-type subjects and at least 3 elective-type subjects are present anywhere in the plan.
- User story: As a student nearing the end of my Diploma, I expect the "Ready to Graduate" banner to appear exactly when I've met LPAB's stated 17+3 requirement **so that** I have an accurate, trustworthy graduation signal.
- Implementation: `engine.js:101-104`; hardcoded thresholds `17`/`3` (C-018).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) 16 compulsory + 3 electives → not ready. (b) 17 compulsory + 2 electives → not ready. (c) 17 compulsory + 3 electives → ready. (d) 17 compulsory + 4 electives → still ready (">=3", not "==3").
- Use cases: UC-01, UC-03
- References: `engine.js:94-108`.

**REQ-012** — `group`/`type` field consistency (architectural guidance)
- Tier: 5 (inferred — no explicit contract states this, but the entire progress/display system implicitly depends on it)
- Summary: Every subject's `group` field (`'core'|'compulsory'|'elective'`, used by all UI display/styling logic) and `type` field (`'Compulsory'|'Elective'`, used exclusively by `engine.js`'s graduation-progress math) must always agree on whether a subject counts as compulsory or elective.
- Specificity: **architectural-guidance** — not independently testable against one code location (it's a cross-cutting data-integrity invariant spanning `subjects.js`'s data plus every consumer of both fields), but essential context for why `engine.js` and the UI never actually diverge today despite reading different fields.
- User story: As a maintainer adding a new subject to `subjects.js`, I need `group` and `type` to agree **so that** the progress bar (which trusts `type`) and the visual styling/labels (which trust `group`) never tell the student two different things about the same subject.
- Implementation note: no code enforces this; it is currently true only because the 27-row dataset happens to be entered consistently.
- Use cases: UC-03, UC-08
- References: `subjects.js` (all rows); `engine.js:98-99`; `ui-board.js:155-158,191-192`; `ui-pool.js:54-57,78-79`.

### Persistence and State Management

**REQ-013** — Autosave on every mutation
- Tier: 4 (README: "Auto-save — Plan is saved to localStorage and persists across page reloads") / Tier 3
- Summary: Every successful plan mutation (`addSubject`, `removeSubject`, `markCompleted`, `resetPlanOnly`) must persist to `localStorage` before the function returns, with no explicit "save" action required from the user.
- User story: As a student, I expect my plan to still be there if I close and reopen my browser **so that** I never lose my planning work.
- Implementation: `planner-state.js` — `savePlan(_plan)` called inside `addSubject` (line 83), `removeSubject` (line 97), `markCompleted` (line 106), `resetPlanOnly` (line 118).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) After any successful add/remove/complete/reset, reloading `loadPlan()` (simulating a page reload) reflects the change. (b) A failed add (validation error) does NOT persist any change.
- Use cases: UC-01, UC-02, UC-03, UC-07
- References: `planner-state.js` throughout; `storage.js:20-36`.

**REQ-014** — Storage failure resilience
- Tier: 3
- Summary: Every `localStorage` read/write must be wrapped so that a thrown exception (private browsing mode, quota exceeded, storage disabled) degrades to a safe default (empty plan / empty hidden list) rather than crashing the app.
- User story: As a student using a locked-down or private browser, I expect the app to still load and function (without persistence) **so that** a storage restriction doesn't make the tool unusable.
- Implementation: `storage.js` — every exported function has a try/catch.
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) If `localStorage.getItem` throws, `loadPlan()` returns `{completed: []}` without propagating the exception. (b) If `localStorage.setItem` throws, `savePlan` logs a warning and returns without throwing.
- Use cases: UC-01 through UC-08 (foundational — every use case depends on the app not crashing on storage errors)
- References: `storage.js:1-80`.

**REQ-015** — Two-step destructive reset confirmation
- Tier: 4 (README doesn't document this explicitly, but it's a clear, deliberate UX contract in the code's own comments) / Tier 3
- Summary: Neither the "clear plan" nor "start over" reset action may execute until the user has (1) selected which reset type they want and (2) explicitly confirmed on a second screen; a backdrop click or Cancel at any point aborts with zero state change.
- User story: As a student who might click Reset by accident, I expect a clear two-step confirmation before anything is destroyed **so that** I can't lose my plan with a single misclick.
- Implementation: `ui-toolbar.js:54-141` (`showResetModal`).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) Clicking "Clear current plan" shows a confirmation screen and does NOT yet clear anything. (b) Clicking "Go back" from the confirmation screen returns to the choice screen with no state change. (c) Only clicking "Yes, continue" on the confirmation screen executes the reset. (d) "Clear current plan" preserves hidden-subject preferences; "Start over entirely" clears them too and reloads the page.
- Use cases: UC-07
- References: `ui-toolbar.js:40-141`.

### Onboarding

**REQ-016** — First-run wizard gating
- Tier: 3
- Summary: The first-run setup wizard must show exactly once, gated by the `lpab_onboarding_done` flag, and must be skippable gracefully if its DOM container is missing.
- User story: As a first-time visitor, I expect to see the setup wizard once **so that** I can choose how to initialize my plan, without seeing it again on every subsequent visit.
- Implementation: `ui-main.js:27-33` (`init`), `ui-onboarding.js:38-46` (`showOnboardingIfNeeded`).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) `isOnboardingDone()` false → wizard shown. (b) `isOnboardingDone()` true → `startApp()` called directly, no wizard. (c) Missing `#onboarding-overlay` → `onComplete` still called (app starts) rather than hanging.
- Use cases: UC-01
- References: `ui-main.js:27-35`; `ui-onboarding.js:38-46`.

**REQ-017** — Onboarding placement failures must surface (currently unmet)
- Tier: 5 (inferred — a reasonable UX expectation, not documented, but strongly implied by the existence of the identical check-and-report pattern already used in `handleAddSubject`)
- Summary: When the onboarding wizard automatically places subjects on the student's behalf (Path A's suggested-pathway apply, Path B's completed-subject batch add), any placement failure must be surfaced to the student, consistent with how manual drag-and-drop placement failures are surfaced.
- User story: As a student choosing "Starting fresh," I expect to be told if any subject from the suggested pathway couldn't be placed **so that** I don't finish onboarding with a silently incomplete plan.
- Implementation note: **Currently unmet.** `applyPathway` (`ui-onboarding.js:157-165`) and the Path B confirm handler (`ui-onboarding.js:220-226`) both call `PlannerState.addSubject` in a loop and discard the return value.
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) If any subject in `SUGGESTED_PATHWAY` fails to add (e.g. hypothetically exceeding 4-per-semester, or a term-availability mismatch), the student sees a specific notice naming which subject(s) failed. (b) Currently: no such notice exists; failures are silently swallowed.
- Use cases: UC-01
- References: `ui-onboarding.js:157-165,197-226`.

### Subject Pool Display

**REQ-018** — Pool excludes placed and hidden subjects
- Tier: 3
- Summary: The subject pool sidebar must exclude any subject already present anywhere in the plan, and separately must move any subject the student has explicitly hidden into a collapsible "Hidden" section rather than the main pool list.
- User story: As a student, I expect the pool to only show subjects I can still add **so that** I'm not tempted to drag a subject that's already placed and would be rejected as a duplicate.
- Implementation: `ui-pool.js:14-23`.
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) A subject placed in any semester (or completed) does not appear in the pool at all (not even in the hidden section). (b) A hidden-but-not-placed subject appears only under "Hidden subjects (N)". (c) Restoring a hidden subject returns it to the main pool list on next render.
- Use cases: UC-01, UC-02

### Exam Date Display

**REQ-019** — Exam-clash warning display
- Tier: 3
- Summary: A subject slot on the board must render every warning string returned for it by `PlannerState.getClashes`, styled distinctly (`slot--clash` class) from non-clashing slots.
- User story: As a student, I expect a clashing subject's tile to visually stand out **so that** I notice the conflict without having to read every warning line.
- Implementation: `ui-board.js:152-160,186-189` (`createSubjectSlot`).
- Use cases: UC-04
- References: `ui-board.js:152-213`.

**REQ-020** — Exam text fallback chain
- Tier: 3
- Summary: For a non-current, non-completed semester, the displayed exam text must prefer the subject's own future-term exam date if set; otherwise it must search `historicalExams` from most-recent to oldest term and show the first match found; otherwise it shows "Exam: TBA".
- User story: As a student looking at a future semester, I expect to see either that subject's actual scheduled exam date, or at least its most recent historical exam date as a rough guide **so that** I have some basis for judging potential clashes even before the official timetable for that term is published.
- Implementation: `ui-board.js:19-43` (`getExamText`).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) A future-term subject with its own non-null `exam` shows that exam string with `(TermName)` suffix. (b) A future-term subject with `exam: null` and a match in `historicalExams` shows the most recent historical entry. (c) A future-term subject with `exam: null` and no historical match shows "Exam: TBA". (d) **[CB-2 gap]** A malformed-but-non-null exam string is shown verbatim as if valid — see REQ-007(d).
- Use cases: UC-05
- References: `ui-board.js:19-43`; `archive.js:1-12`.

### Export

**REQ-021** — Markdown export completeness and ordering
- Tier: 4 (README: "Markdown export — One-click copy of the full plan") / Tier 3
- Summary: The exported markdown must include a `## Completed` section (if non-empty) followed by every other semester key present in the plan object, each as its own `## <Term Name>` heading listing every subject with lecture (and exam, if present) detail.
- User story: As a student pasting my plan into Notion, I expect every semester I've touched to appear, in a sensible order **so that** the exported document is a complete, readable record of my plan.
- Implementation: `ui-toolbar.js:15-38` (`handleExport`).
- Conditions of satisfaction: (a) Every non-empty semester key in the plan (other than `completed`) appears as its own section. (b) Each subject line includes id, name, lecture, and exam (if not null). (c) **[Ordering gap — see CB-6/BUGS.md]** Semesters are sorted **alphabetically** (`Object.keys(plan)...sort()`, `ui-toolbar.js:26`), not chronologically — this produces a non-chronological reading order whenever the plan spans a year boundary in certain configurations (e.g., `'summer2030'` sorts before `'winter2029'` alphabetically despite `winter2029` occurring first in real time). Recorded as the documented actual behavior; Phase 3/4 determines whether this is a defect worth fixing.
- Use cases: UC-06
- References: `ui-toolbar.js:15-38`.
